About

I’m a physicist working at the interface of the life sciences and AI, focusing on cardiac mechanics. I study how heart muscle cells are built and how they contract, in health, in disease models, and under drug treatment. To do this, I develop methods for cardiac phenomics that measure structure and function at scale, from single sarcomeres in beating stem-cell-derived heart cells to tissue models. I pair these with dynamic models of contraction (eLife). At the core is SarcAsM, my AI-based software for multiscale analysis of sarcomere structure and contractility. I’ve also worked on epithelial morphogenesis in fruit-fly embryos, combining deep-learning image analysis (DeepProjection) with vertex models of tissue mechanics (PNAS).

Since 2025, I’ve been a data scientist and AI engineer at TECLION, the AI competence center of the Hessian tax administration. There, I build agentic applications, retrieval-augmented knowledge systems, and voice pipelines for public administration. We do this under strict security standards: tax data is highly sensitive, so instead of plugging into commercial APIs, we use and adapt open-weight models and run them in the administration’s own secure infrastructure. My focus is often the harness, meaning the evaluation, validation, and tooling that make agents reliable enough to trust. I continue my research part-time as a postdoc at UMG Göttingen.
